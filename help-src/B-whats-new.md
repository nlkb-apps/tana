# What's new

## 2026-10-13: Today's Box · 今日の一箱

A new daily game. Every day at midnight Japan time, one box opens for everyone as an 8-bit mosaic. Guess the game in six tries, and each miss sharpens the box and adds a clue.

Every day you finish earns a stamp on the month's stamp card. Stamp a whole month and its brass plate goes under your shelf. Share your result without spoiling the answer, and see how the people you follow did. No account needed to play: tana.gamingjapanese.com/box/. See Today's Box for how it works.

## 2026-10-13: Box art for PS3, Switch and newer consoles

Box art now covers far more of the catalogue. PS3 and Switch games get their real boxes for each region, matched by product code where Tana has one, and PS4, PS5, Xbox 360 and Xbox One games mostly have art too. Games filed under a capitalised or catalogue-style title ("Elder Scrolls V, The - Skyrim"), special editions and games known by their Japanese name now find their art, and when the same game is filed once per platform, the entries share it. Nintendo Switch 2 Editions use the Switch box until they have their own.

In My 9, each version of a game now shows its box when you pick the one you played.

## 2026-10-12: Box art

Games now show their real box art: on game pages, next to every release, in search results, in My 9 and in the pictures you share. More than three quarters of all releases have it already, matched by product code where possible. Each box keeps a small JP, NA or EU patch for the region of the release, so it's clear which one you're looking at even when the art comes from another region.

Games with no art yet show a plain label with the title. An approved photo of the front of your copy fills it in, and you can report wrong box art from the game page. See Box art for where it comes from.

## 2026-10-12: My 9 · 私を構成する9本

Make the grid of the nine games that made you, on a 3 by 3 cubby shelf. Pick the exact version you played, add your age or the year and a line about why, and put your keystone game in the middle. Each game shows its box art.

Anyone can build one at tana.gamingjapanese.com/my9/ without an account. Saving, sharing a link and comparing with other people need a free account, and Tana keeps your grid while you sign up. The census shows what everyone is picking, Japan and North America side by side. See My 9 for how it all works.

## 2026-10-12: Wants and lists, with nine boxes

Wants is now Wants & lists. Your wantlist is drawn as a shelf, with an Order menu and a green tick on anything you already own. Next to it you have nine boxes, numbered like the boxes in a Pokémon PC, for any games at all, owned or not: what to play next, what you finished this year, a series you're following, your shopping list for the next trip.

Each box has its own name, note, order and style. Fill a box by searching inside it, or tap Add to a box on any game page. Visitors to a public collection can see the wantlist and boxes under a new Wants & lists tab on the profile. See Boxes for how it all works.

## 2026-10-12: Shelf styles and smaller shelves

You can now choose what your shelf is made of. Tap Style above the shelf for ten woods (from oak and walnut to hinoki and ebony), seven metals, glossy lacquer or paint, in a long list of colors or one you mix yourself. Visitors see it too, on your shelf, your share page and the shelf picture.

You can also make up to five smaller shelves next to your main one: your favourites, what you're playing, your grails. Each has its own name, style and order, and you pick the games from your collection. They show up in a row above your shelf, and on your share page under the main shelf. See Smaller shelves for how it works.

## 2026-10-12: The Discord server is open

When you join the Tana Discord, it now asks what you collect and which platforms you want to hear about, then shows you the channels for them. You can change your picks any time in Channels & Roles. The invite link is now discord.gg/MXWmYAhxUs and doesn't expire; the old link stops working at the end of October.

## 2026-10-11: Arrange your shelf

You can now drag the boxes on your shelf into any order you like, and Tana keeps it as My own order. There are new ways to order the shelf too: release date, series and region, as well as console, title and recently added, with a small label on the shelf edge for each group. Your order is saved to your account and is what visitors see on your public shelf.

The shelf itself got a polish: real wood grain, a light over each shelf, cases that look like their real counterparts, a band on copies with their obi or spine card, and a shine on sealed copies. Hover over a box to see your photo of it.

Boxes now show the English or romanised name by default, with a small JP, NA or EU sticker for the edition. Choose Japanese titles in Account if you'd rather see Japanese on the spines. Share pages and their pictures follow the same rule.

## 2026-10-07: A new look, and quicker ways to add games

The front page now shows what Tana is in one go: a sample shelf, a link to see a real one, and the search box right there. When you're signed in, it opens with your own newest boxes and shortcuts to scan, tick off a platform or import a list.

You can now add a game from search with one tap on the circle next to it, and game pages have Add to collection, Want it and Share at the top. Picking an edition is a single tap from a list of every edition. Your collection page shows your numbers at a glance, has an Add games button with every way to add in one place, and can sort your shelf by newest first. On a computer, the menu moves to the top bar and pages use the wider screen.

## 2026-10-06: Game-Key Cards for new Western releases

New Western-only Switch 2 games are now marked as either a full game card or a Game-Key Card when they're added, the same way Japanese releases already were.

## 2026-10-05: New games from everywhere, and Recently added

Tana now looks for new Western releases every week as well as Japanese ones, and games that were announced before they came out are now added once they're released. The home page shows the games most recently added to Tana, from every region, newest first.

## 2026-09-29: Help pages

These help pages are now live at tana.gamingjapanese.com/help/, with a link at the top of every public page and at the bottom of the app.

## 2026-09-28: Discord server and subreddit

Tana now has a Discord server (discord.gg/MXWmYAhxUs) and a subreddit, r/tanashelf. You'll find links on the Community page and at the bottom of every page.

## 2026-09-27: Cover variants and edition notes

You can now add a Cover variant when a game was printed with different box art under the same serial, and describe what makes an edition different. Comments on game pages also count toward your contribution orb now.

## 2026-09-25: Sharing, Community and the leaderboard

Your collection now has its own share link with a picture of your shelf, a shelf image you can download, and embed code. There's a new Community tab with the leaderboard, a Following feed and wants matches, and game pages show how many collectors own and want each edition.

## 2026-09-25: Asia and Korea

Asian and Korean releases now have their own regions, in ochre and plum.

## 2026-09-25: More editions

Serials and European country editions for PS1, PS2, Saturn, Dreamcast and GameCube, and reprint lines like Greatest Hits, Platinum and Player's Choice across most platforms. Japanese edition names now show in Japanese.

## 2026-09-24: The shelf view

Collections now open as a shelf, with real box sizes. The list view is still there.

## 2026-09-24: Western-only games

About 12,000 games that only came out outside Japan were added, along with the Western only and Physical in the West only stamps and filters.
