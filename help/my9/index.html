My 9 · 私を構成する9本

My 9 is the "9 games that made me" grid (私を構成する9本 in Japanese) done properly: nine games in a 3 by 3 cubby shelf, each one the exact version you played, with when it got you and why. Anyone can make one without an account. You need an account to save it, share a link to it and compare it with other people's.

Start at tana.gamingjapanese.com/my9/, or tap Make a My 9 on the home page.

Building your nine

Tap an empty cubby and search any game in the catalogue, in English, Japanese or romaji. Then pick the version you played: the Super Famicom original, the SNES release, the PlayStation port and so on. Each one shows its box art, with a patch for its region. A version with no box art yet shows a plain label with its title instead (see Box art).

For each game you can add:

  • Your age then, or the year you played it
  • A line about why it's here (up to 140 characters). Tick the spoiler box to hide the line behind a cover that people tap to open.
  • If the game is in your collection and you've added a photo, you can show your own copy instead of the box art.

To move games around, drag one cubby onto another to swap them, or use Move to cubby in the game's window.

The keystone

The middle cubby is the keystone: the one game everything else hangs on. It has a gold frame, and the census counts which games people put there.

Prompt, plates, titles and style

  • Prompt changes the heading: The 9 games that made me, 9 games from my childhood, 9 Japan-only gems, 9 games I'd save from a fire, My 9 of the year, or 9 comfort games. Each comes with its Japanese heading.
  • Plates are the small brass labels under each cubby. They show your age, the year, or nothing.
  • Titles can be shown in English or Japanese.
  • Where did you play these? lets you say whether you grew up with Japanese, North American, European or other releases. It feeds the census.
  • Style gives the cubby shelf any wood, metal, lacquer or paint, the same as your shelves.

Without an account

Your grid stays on the device you're using while you build it, and you can download the pictures. When you tap save, share or compare, Tana asks you to create a free account (or sign in). As soon as you're in, your grid is saved to your account and you carry on where you were.

Saving and sharing

A saved My 9 lives in one of your nine boxes under Wants & lists, shown as a small 3 by 3 grid on the board. Open it to edit it whenever you like; changes save as you go. You can have more than one, one per box: a childhood nine, a nine of the year, and so on.

Each one has its own link, tana.gamingjapanese.com/my9/<username>/<box number>/. When you post it, it shows as a picture of your grid. The buttons under the grid copy the link or post it to X or Bluesky with #私を構成する9本 and #My9Games already added.

Download picture gives you a square image for posts, and Download story a tall one for stories, with your lines written out under the grid. Visitors and people who see your post can't change anything. They can tap the games they've played too, which only they see.

Your collection needs to be public for other people to open your My 9 link and for it to count in the census.

Comparing

On someone else's My 9, tap Compare with my 9 to see the games you share, whether you have the same keystone, and the platforms you both picked from. You need an account and a My 9 of your own.

The census

The census at tana.gamingjapanese.com/#/my9/census adds up every public My 9: the most picked games, the most common keystones, platforms, decades, Japan and North America picks side by side, and the biggest gap between them (games one side loves and the other barely knows). When you're signed in, it also shows the people whose nine are nearest to yours.

Turning a box into a My 9

Any box can become a My 9. In Rename, move or delete, tick Make it a 3×3 grid. The first nine games in the box become the grid. Untick it to turn it back into an ordinary box.
